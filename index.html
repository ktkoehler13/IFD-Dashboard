Why unit records are left out

Unit records by reason. Rows marked "kept" are now counted, using the turnout fill-in or the recorded cancel time; the rest have no usable start or end, or are demonstrably wrong, and are excluded. In late May and early June 2024, — false-alarm unit records were left open overnight and closed together the next morning, adding — hours that did not happen; any alarm or no-emergency response committed over 4 hours is now excluded as an error. "Dispatched, no later times" and missing dispatch times jumped in 2026, which points to how the new export or reporting system writes these records rather than to missing work.

Definitions

Incident
One call for service. The exports list one row per unit (2026: per firefighter), so rows are reduced to one per incident before any incident count or incident-level figure, and to one per unit per incident for unit counts.
Comparison window
January 1 through October 17 in every year, the last complete day in the latest 2026 export. Partial years are never compared with full years.
Response time
Turnout (dispatch to en route) plus travel (en route to on scene) for the first engine to arrive (901, 902, 903 or 904). Truck and Command are not counted as the first arrival: the measure is engine to engine. An earlier version of this dashboard included them, which understated the first-due delay (corrected October 23, 2026). Incidents where no engine arrived are counted in the totals but not measured. In-area incidents only (City zones and Town ITH- zones). The export's "Time to First Arrival" field is not used: it starts when the call reaches the dispatch center, before the department is notified.
Excluded records
Negative intervals; turnout over 6 minutes; zero turnout or travel; arrival stamped at departure (on scene 5 seconds or less after a travel time over 9 minutes); missing en-route time. Arrivals over 17 minutes are kept. No value is estimated.
First-due engine committed
The engine assigned to the incident's response zone (99 → 902; 95, 951 → 903; 96, 961 → 904; all others → 901) had been dispatched to another incident and was not yet back in service when this incident was dispatched. Out-of-service time for maintenance or training is not in the data and is not counted.
Response zone fill-in (October 2026)
The October 18 export left the response zone blank on many October incidents, apparently because it is completed later in the records process. By department decision, a blank October response zone takes the zone recorded at the same address on earlier incidents, but only when one zone accounts for at least 90% of that address's history; otherwise it stays blank. This affects first-due and area figures only. By department confirmation, 115 East Shore Cir and 134 Graham Rd (two 2026 structure fires with no zone in the export) are out of district.
Overlap (concurrency)
Another incident was active — dispatched and not yet cleared by its last unit — when this incident was dispatched.
Committed hours
Workload only. Recorded time from dispatch to back in service for each front-line unit, plus an estimated return drive equal to that response's own measured travel time (only where the travel time passes the error checks). A unit canceled before arriving ends at its back-in-service time, or at its recorded cancel time when back in service is blank; its return drive is estimated as the time from en route to cancel (counted only when that is positive and under 20 minutes, and only when back in service is blank or stamped at the cancel). Where a unit record has an en-route time but no dispatch time, the dispatch time is set to en-route minus that position's average 2026 turnout. This is the one place on the dashboard where a missing value is filled rather than excluded, by department decision, and the filled hours are shown separately. Records with no end time, or a span of zero or over 24 hours, are excluded, as are alarm or no-emergency responses committed more than 4 hours (records left open and closed later in a batch). The headline figure is the median minutes per response; the average is shown alongside. Engine 901's projection multiplies its 2026 share of incidents and its 2026 average committed minutes per response by each incident scenario. Availability, first-due and overlap figures do not include the return time or the fill-in.
Incident group
NERIS primary incident group, with two department adjustments: "Medical – Injury / Trauma – Motor Vehicle Collision" is counted as Rescue, because the incident is a vehicle crash (— incidents in 2026); and the remaining medical incidents are split into Illness and Injury/Trauma. Medical turnout is measured against the 60-second EMS benchmark; reclassified crashes use the 80-second benchmark. Groups are what the crew recorded on scene. Dispatch type is not used. Groups are not compared with earlier years.
Projection
2026 to date plus 2025's actual incidents from October 18 to December 31, multiplied by the growth measured over the comparison window.
Five-year projection
Three scenarios from the 2026 year-end projection. Low applies the slowest year-over-year growth since 2022 (same calendar window) each year. Central compounds 2026's growth over the same calendar window of 2025 each year. High applies the compound growth from 2022 to 2026 on the same calendar window. 2021 is excluded because COVID-19 depressed its volume. These are scenarios for planning, not predictions.
